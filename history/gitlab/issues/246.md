The same-version harness (`tests/unit/test_document_fidelity.py`) asserts `D -> model -> D`. There is no equivalent for `D(2.1) -> 3.0 -> D(2.1)`, so the table in #242 was produced by a throwaway script and will go stale the moment anything changes.

## What it should assert

For each 2.1 fixture, one of three outcomes, all *asserted* rather than tolerated — the shape already used by the same-version harness and the conformance gate:

| outcome | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **converts** | the document survives the trip unchanged |
| **declared block** | one leg raises `ValidationError`, and the reason is recorded — the correct answer where SDMX cannot carry the construct |
| **known-lossy** | it survives with recorded differences, each pointing at the issue that tracks them |

Entries in the second and third categories are asserted *still* blocked or *still* lossy, so removing a bottleneck fails the test until its entry goes. That is what makes the burn-down real rather than decorative.

## Why it is worth building rather than re-measuring by hand

Two of the three current blocks are ours and are being removed (#243, #244). Without a standing harness, the first sign that a conversion has started silently losing something would be nobody noticing — which is the failure this whole line of work exists to prevent.

It also documents the *permanent* boundaries in executable form. The grouping containers (`StructureSet`, `HierarchicalCodelist`) and, probably, the MSD reporting layer (#245) can never make the trip, because the intermediate 3.0 document has nowhere to hold them. Recording those as declared blocks states the boundary and stops it being re-litigated.

## Scope notes

- Both directions are worth covering — `3.0 -> 2.1 -> 3.0` has its own asymmetries (multiple measures, `Metadataflow` targets, non-`OBS_VALUE` measure ids) that are already refused and should be asserted as such.
- The comparison should reuse `tests.support.fidelity`, whose equivalences (identity keying, XSD defaults, redundant urns, the excluded message header) are as applicable here as in the same-version case.
- 3.1 is worth including once 3.0 is green; it differs from 3.0 in a small, known set (prohibited `Categorisation/@version`, no `AttachmentConstraint`).
