## Measured on `src/sdmxlib`

| | sdmxlib | pinax (before) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Sphinx/RST roles (`` :class:`X` ``) | **0** | 98 |
| Non-Google docstrings (pydoclint DOC003) | **0** | 13 |

So the convention holds — by discipline, with nothing checking it. That is the shape
CLAUDE.md already calls out for the basedpyright rules: *"Zero findings when enabled; on
as regression guards."* This is the docstring equivalent.

## Why ruff does not already cover it

`ruff.toml` sets `convention = "google"` under `select = ["ALL"]`, and that is genuinely
not enough. Ruff's pydocstyle checks a docstring's **structure** — summary line, blank
lines, punctuation — and never reads its markup or rejects a NumPy section header. In
pinax that setting sat declared and unenforced for the life of the file while 98 roles and
13 NumPy-sectioned docstrings accumulated underneath it.

Worth knowing because it is counter-intuitive: the config looks like the gate, and is not.

## What pinax landed (pinax!77)

`just check-docstrings`, wired into `check`, in three arms:

1. **No Sphinx roles** — a grep. No tool in the ecosystem models this; I checked
   pydoclint and griffe and both parse ``:class:`X` `` as ordinary prose, correctly,
   because banning a markup dialect is a house policy rather than a correctness property.
2. **No NumPy section underlines** — matched as a section *name* followed by a rule.
3. **pydoclint `DOC003`** (`--check-style-mismatch=True`, off by default) — catches a
   docstring whose shape is not Google even without an underline.

### The gotcha that will bite you specifically

Arm 2 must match a section **name plus** a rule, never a bare rule. sdmxlib has prose
headings in module docstrings — `_dto.py:10` ("Conventions for wire Structs") and
`metadata_annotation.py:14` / `:34` — which are perfectly good RST-flavoured headings that
a naive `^\s*-{3,}$` grep flags as NumPy sections. That false positive cost me a round in
pinax; you would hit it immediately on those three.

## Cost, honestly

DOC003 alone is **0 findings** on sdmxlib — pure regression guard, adopt for free.

The rest of pydoclint is not free. Same invocation, all codes: **562 findings** — 170
`DOC203` (return type disagrees with the annotation), 85 `DOC103` (argument mismatch), 82
`DOC101`, 80 `DOC201`, plus `DOC601`/`DOC603` class-attribute checks. Whether any of that
is worth having is a separate question from the gate, and I am not arguing it here; pinax
tracks its own 55 as a follow-up (pinax#108) rather than blocking the convention work on
them.

So the offer is narrow: **DOC003 + the two greps**, which is 0 findings and stops drift.

Recipe and config are in pinax!77 if you want to lift them; the pydoclint settings that
matter are `check-style-mismatch = true` and `arg-type-hints-in-docstring = false` (the
default demands type hints inside the docstring text, which neither project does).

Happy to open an MR here if useful, or leave it as a note if you would rather not carry
another dev dependency for one check.

!483 — taken. Re-measured every claim first and all three hold: **0** Sphinx
roles, **0** NumPy sections, **0** DOC003. Pure regression guard, adopted for

Four things the offer could not have known:

**One arm already existed.** `just check-docstrings` was already in `check` as
a grep for `:class:`/`:meth:`/`:func:`/`:attr:`/`Example::`. So it was two arms
to build, not three — and the existing one covered four roles out of the
dialect. Widened to `:exc:`, `:ref:`, `:doc:`, `:term:`, `:const:`,
`:keyword:`, `:envvar:`, `:option:` and the `py:`-prefixed spellings, on the
grounds that a guard banning only what already appeared bans nothing.

**Your false positive was exactly right.** `_dto.py:11` and
`metadata_annotation.py:15`/`:35`, those three and no others. Arm 2 keys on a
section *name* followed by a rule, and both headings are in the tests verbatim
so it cannot be loosened back without going red.

**DOC003 earns its dependency, but not for the stated reason.** The offer
describes it as catching a docstring "whose shape is not Google even without an
underline" — measured, it does *not* fire on a NumPy docstring with no rule.
What it catches, and neither grep can, is a **Sphinx field-list** docstring:
`:param x:` / `:type x:` / `:returns:` carries no role and no underline. That
is the gap, and it is the better argument for the dependency.

**It shipped broken and the sabotage caught it.** `style_mismatches` first read
`proc.stdout`; pydoclint 0.9 writes findings to **stderr** and leaves stdout
empty, so the arm reported a clean tree on a file it had just flagged. Nothing
in the output distinguished that from working — which is the hazard specific to
a gate that starts at zero, and the reason each arm was sabotaged rather than
eyeballed. A test now asserts the arm consults stderr, checked against the
source, because running pydoclint cannot tell "no findings" from "not looking".

Also: CLAUDE.md gains the convention. The old recipe comment said "(see
CLAUDE.md)" and CLAUDE.md said nothing about docstrings — the rule lived only
in the grep enforcing part of it.

Not adopted, per your framing: pydoclint's other codes, **602** on this tree
(179 DOC203, 96 DOC103, 93 DOC101, 86 DOC201, plus the DOC5xx/DOC6xx families).
Same discipline as `check_dead_ignores.py` — run a whole checker, consult one
rule. Worth its own issue here if you ever want it, mirroring your pinax#108.

Thanks for the offer and for the config details; `check-style-mismatch` and
`arg-type-hints-in-docstring=false` were both needed exactly as you said.
