## Summary

Where 2.1 and 3.x model the *same concept with a different structure*, the
model currently holds **both shapes** (a union). The writers then drop or
refuse whichever shape the target format lacks. For a 3.x-native model, each
pair needs one canonical 3.x shape plus a declared **conversion** for 2.1:
convert on 2.1 ingest, project on 2.1 write, and refuse only where no
mapping exists. This is m12's principle ("convert what converts, refuse only
what cannot"), applied to the shapes m12 left as union.

## Order

Measures and codelist extensions first: they change what a 2.1 consumer
*sees* (different keys; missing codes), not just provenance. Then hierarchies
and maps (a synthetic artefact appears on round trip). Then constraints,
which depend on #69.
