Closes #473: accepted and documented, by the maintainer's decision.

**The trade.** Since #461 an SDMX-JSON read finds its losses when `StructureMessage.lost` is first read, and the message keeps the document's bytes until then. That's **bounded at one copy** of the document the caller parsed, and it only adds memory once the caller has dropped its own copy. The alternatives, measured on current `main`:

| option | cost |
| --- | --- |
| walk eagerly (no retention) | every JSON parse pays, mostly for callers who never read `lost`: UNSD 178 → 342 ms (**+92%**), `CL_GEOGRAPHY` **+1.2 s** |
| keep only the parts with skipped keys | needs to know during the typed decode which structs skipped keys; `msgspec` has no catch-all field, short of a parallel family of strict structs |

**What changes (docs and tests only):**
- `StructureMessage.lost`: the trade, the bound, and the two ways to release it: read `lost`, or `parse(..., lossless=True)`.
- `parse`'s `lossless` argument: the same, from the caller's side.
- `docs/upgrading.md`: a bullet under "What a read cannot hold is reported", naming the case that matters, a process that keeps many parsed JSON messages alive.
- `tests/unit/test_json_lazy_losses.py`: two tests hold the release claims. Reading `lost` lets the document go, and a lossless parse keeps nothing to release.
