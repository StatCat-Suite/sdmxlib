## The measurement came first

`tests/support/fidelity.py` compares `D` against `serialize(parse(D), same_version)` by element *identity* rather than position. Getting the comparison right was most of the work, and three of its rules are claims about what the formats say two documents mean:

- **The message `Header` is excluded.** The schemas document every field as describing the transmission — "an identification for the message, assigned by the sender", "the date the message was prepared" — not the artefacts. Parsing and re-serialising produces a *new* message, and giving it a new header is correct. Excluding it took the corpus from 0/22 clean to 14/22.
- **Indistinguishable siblings are keyed by content, not position.** Cube-region `<Value>`s have nothing to key on, and positional keys turned a reordering into 139 phantom differences. Now a multiset: reordering matches, a changed / dropped / de-duplicated value does not. Nothing in the schemas gives meaning to such a list's order — where SDMX cares it uses an explicit `position` attribute.
- **Redundant `urn`s, XSD-defaulted attributes and `xs:decimal` spellings are equivalent.** `15` and `15.0` are the same number; `maxOccurs="1"` where the default is 1 is the same document.

## Why the containers are modelled rather than flattened onto their contents

The alternative — `container_id` / `container_name` fields on each contained artefact — **denormalises a 1:N relationship into its N children**. Nothing would stop the copies disagreeing, so the model could represent states no document can express, and reconstructing the grouping on write becomes a derivation rather than a fact. It also captures two of the container's eight fields, so it would have shortened the gap list without closing it, and taxed every 3.0 user with fields that are always `None`.

Modelling them as peers that *reference* their contents keeps one copy of the grouping, leaves the 3.0 view untouched, and makes 2.1 → 2.1 lossless across every field.

**This is not a new pattern.** `AttachmentConstraint` is already a 2.1-only artefact modelled as a first-class message field and refused for 3.0/3.1/JSON via `_UNWRITABLE`. The three are now consistent rather than three answers to one question.

## Controls

Every fix has a mutation verified to fail — 5 for the containers, 3 for the NCName set, 4 for the comparator's multiset behaviour, plus the earlier slices. Controls clear `__pycache__` first: `git checkout` restores an older mtime, so Python reuses bytecode compiled from the mutated source and a control reports a false pass.
