Three open questions from step 4's sizing, answered against the model
rather than guessed.

**The key is the dimensions.** Observations have no surrogate key, but
they are not unkeyed: the information model identifies an observation by
its dimension values. So `__key__` is `dimension_column_map` in DSD
order and keyset paging works for the same reason it works structurally.

**`ids()`/`urns()` should be absent, not raising.** `Query`'s existing
precedent — no resolver, so `.objects` raises — does not transfer, and
the difference is the point: whether a resolver exists is data-dependent
and may differ between processes, while whether a root has an id is a
property of its type, known statically. This codebase already argues it
in NOT_EDGES, where an edge was declined because "an affordance that
lies is worse than a plain column". A splittable `Query` /
`KeyedQuery` costs two class statements, because every refining method
returns `Self`, plus an overload on the root.

**Column types come from the DSD, not DESCRIBE.** A component's
representation is a `Ref[Codelist]` or a `Facet` whose `type` is one of
`DataType`'s 39 members — exactly the encoding question. Measured on a
DSD built three ways: an enumerated dimension, one inheriting
`ObservationalTimePeriod` from its concept (the IM's fallback, already
implemented in `Component.__attrs_post_init__`), one with no
representation at all, and a `Double` measure.

Three consequences. DESCRIBE could not have answered — a parquet column
of codes is VARCHAR whatever it means, and only the DSD says *enumerated
by CL_GEO*, which is what would later let a dimension reach its
codelist. The IM's inheritance needs no new code. And the DSD does not
always answer, so the mapping needs an unknown arm, and it should be
permissive: a dataflow the library cannot type is still one a caller can
query.

Statically `obs["GEO"]` is one signature, so the permissive `Col` form
stays for dynamic ids and `obs.num("OBS_VALUE")` gives an exact type
with the DSD checked at the call — the caller states the expectation and
the model verifies it, the bargain `type[T]` witnesses make elsewhere.

§5 also gains the fourth coupling the original survey missed, since a
doc claiming three probes while four were found is the kind of drift
that makes the next reader trust the wrong number.
