CLAUDE.md states twice that a finding which cannot fail is one nobody acts on.
Both statements are about writing a guard correctly *to begin with*. Neither
covers the case that kept happening: a guard that **was** correct, **was**
watched to fail, and later stopped being able to — nothing it asserts changed,
its *subject* moved.

The new section names four mechanisms, each from a real instance this week, and
the design choice that prevents each:

1. **Assert the exact quantity when it is knowable.** The one guard that caught
   itself did so because it asserts `sites == len(_MARKERS)` and failed at
   `0 == 30`; a `>=` bound would have passed in silence.
2. **A comparison needs a discrimination check** — `sdmx_ml30` against
   `sdmx_ml31` became trivially satisfied once both were 41-line bindings.
3. **A guard that starts at zero needs a sabotage test** — green and broken are
   the same output, which is how a pydoclint arm parsing an empty stream
   reported a clean tree on a flagged file.
4. **Name the subject, not a proxy for it** — `len(again) > 20` stood in for
   "the cascade ran" and stopped meaning that when the cascade got cheaper.

## The audit, and eight tightened

Filing a rule without applying it is half the job. Surveyed all 46 bound-style
assertions on counts under `tests/`:

* **33 carry a justification** and are correct — discovery floors ("the walk
  has gone blind"), per-item budgets, ratios. Those are the exemption rule 1
  names, correctly applied.
* **11 carried no message.** Three are `n_bytes > 0` smoke checks in the bench
  tier and stay. The other eight were bounds on quantities that are perfectly
  knowable, and are now exact:

| was | now | what the bound admitted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `len(msg.codelists) >= 1` ×4 | `== _EXPECTED[fixture,...]` | `with_groups` losing two of its three codelists |
| `len(plain) > 1` | `== 3` | a plan dropping a code from an unsorted aggregate |
| `len(whole.columns) > 1` | `== 13` | the relation silently narrowing |
| `len(params) <= 5` | `== 2` | a rewrite binding four, under a comment that already called it a *fixed* set |
| `len(exc.value.issues) >= 2` | `== 2` | one codelist producing both issues and the other none |

The JSON-reader four are the clearest: the fixture counts differ (1, 3, 2
codelists) and were all being checked against `>= 1`.

## Not proposed

An automated check. *Is this assertion still capable of failing* is not
decidable, and mutation testing is the nearest tool — #324's subject, and out
of `just ci` on cost. This is a written rule and a review question, like the
two sentences it extends.

Closes #403.
