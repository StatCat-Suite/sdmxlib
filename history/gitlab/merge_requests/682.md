Step 4 of #516: `obs.pivot(on=None) -> pl.DataFrame`.

**Choosing the axis:**
- The axis is `on=` if given. Otherwise it is the DSD's `MeasureDimension`. `on=` replaces `Dataset`'s `measure_dim=` hint, as decided.
- With no axis (no `on=` and no measure dimension), or with `on=` naming something that isn't a dimension, it raises a `ValueError` listing the enumerated dimensions that could be the axis.

**Columns:**
- Columns follow the structure: the concept scheme for a measure dimension, the codelist for a dimension. A value with no rows after a filter is still a column.
- Only when the enumeration is unresolved are the columns read from the data, sorted.

**Index:** every other dimension, then the time dimension. That includes the measure dimension when pivoting `on=` a regular one. `Dataset.pivot` dropped the measure dimension from the index in that case, so EMP and UNEMP rows of one series collided. With that line removed, the `on=` test fails.

**Ordering:** rows come in `frame()` order (key order), with `maintain_order=True`.

**Return type:** `pl.DataFrame`, not `Dataset.pivot`'s `LazyFrame`. A pivot needs the whole frame anyway, and every other `ObsQuery` terminal returns eager frames.

This depends on !681 (a binding from a DSD now maps the measure dimension).
