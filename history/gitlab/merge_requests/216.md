## `_binding` was two different things wearing one name

Six derive an identity binding from a DSD — four of them byte-identical:

```python
DataflowBinding.from_dsd(dsd, dataflow=Ref[Dataflow].to(flow), schema="data", table="obs")
```

The other four are hand-built `DataflowBinding(...)` literals carrying partition layouts, structural versions, sidecars and materialisation timestamps. They exist to exercise **the binding itself** — drift detection, store inspection — not to feed a writer. Folding them together would have merged a fixture with a subject.

`dsd_binding` takes a `dataflow_ref` override, which absorbs the one variant that toggled resolved/unresolved.

## `_make_msd` is the clearest drift yet

Seven modules built an `MSD_FOOTNOTES` for `STC_CP` with a `FOOTNOTE_TEXT` attribute, and each set whichever optional fields it happened to need: a name here, a description there, occurs bounds, a concept ref, a `max_length`, and one with twenty annotated attributes.

Two were byte-identical and are now `footnote_msd`. **The other five keep their own builder** — those extras are what those tests are *about*, and a factory with six optional knobs would be harder to read than the duplication it removed.

Same verdict as !214 reached for the five remaining `_make_dsd` definitions. A builder used by one test is not duplication, and a shared module that accumulates them becomes a dumping ground.
