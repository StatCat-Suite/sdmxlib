Taking the offer. Every claim in it re-measured on this tree first, and all
three hold: **0** Sphinx roles, **0** NumPy sections, **0** pydoclint DOC003.
So this is a pure regression guard, the shape CLAUDE.md already describes for
the basedpyright rules — *zero findings when enabled; on as regression guards*.

## One arm already existed

`just check-docstrings` was already in `check` as a grep for
`:class:`/`:meth:`/`:func:`/`:attr:`/`Example::`. The offer assumes three arms
to build; it was two. What is added:

* **Arm 1 widened** to the whole role dialect — `:exc:`, `:ref:`, `:doc:`,
  `:term:`, `:const:`, `:keyword:`, `:envvar:`, `:option:` and the `py:`-prefixed
  spellings. A guard that bans only what already appeared bans nothing, and the
  roles that had never occurred are exactly the ones that would slip in.
* **Arm 2**, NumPy section headers.
* **Arm 3**, pydoclint `DOC003` via `--check-style-mismatch`.

## The predicted false positive, and what it cost

#374 warned that a naive `^-{3,}$` grep flags `_dto.py:11` and
`metadata_annotation.py:15`/`:35`, which head **prose** with an RST rule and
are perfectly good Google docstrings. Confirmed — those three lines and no
others. Arm 2 keys on a section *name* followed by a rule, and both headings
are in the tests verbatim so the rule cannot be loosened back without failing.

## Arm 3 earns its dependency, but not for the stated reason

The offer describes DOC003 as catching "a docstring whose shape is not Google
even without an underline". Measured: it does **not** fire on a NumPy docstring
with no rule. What it does catch, and neither grep can, is a **Sphinx
field-list** docstring —

    :param x: A thing.
    :type x: int
    :returns: The thing.

— which carries no role and no underline. That is the gap it fills, and it is
the reason to accept the dependency rather than the one offered.

## The arm shipped broken, and the sabotage is why that is in the past tense

`style_mismatches` first read `proc.stdout`. pydoclint 0.9 writes findings to
**stderr** and leaves stdout empty, so the arm reported a clean tree on a file
pydoclint had just flagged. Nothing about the output said so — a gate that
starts at zero prints the same green whether it works or not, which is exactly
why every arm here was sabotaged rather than eyeballed.
`test_the_pydoclint_arm_reads_the_stream_pydoclint_writes_to` keeps it fixed,
asserted against the source because running pydoclint cannot distinguish "no
findings" from "not looking".

## Not adopted

pydoclint's other codes: **602** findings on this tree — 179 `DOC203`, 96
`DOC103`, 93 `DOC101`, 86 `DOC201`, plus the `DOC5xx`/`DOC6xx` families.
Whether any of it is worth having is a separate question this gate takes no
position on, the same way `check_dead_ignores.py` runs a whole checker and
consults one rule.

## Also

CLAUDE.md gains the convention. The recipe's old comment said "(see CLAUDE.md)"
and CLAUDE.md said nothing about docstrings — the rule lived only in the grep
that enforced part of it.

`tools/check_docstrings.py` matches the finding profile of the existing
`tools/check_dead_ignores.py` under ruff (D103, INP001, S607, T201). `just
lint` covers `src tests bench`, not `tools/`, which is its own question.
