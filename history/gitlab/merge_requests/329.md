The comparison #218 asked for has been unanswerable for three
milestones. #314 made it runnable; this runs it.

`tools/mutate_compare.py` puts two test selections against the same
module and the same mutants and reports the *asymmetry*, because two
percentages settle nothing — both suites are kept whatever the split.
What decides something is which mutants each suite catches alone.

Over `_compile.py`, restricted to the 742 mutants both arms execute:

    killed only by properties     0
    killed only by examples     106
    killed by both              440
    killed by neither           196

The properties killed a strict subset — every mutant they caught, the
examples caught too. That is a sharper result than "fewer", and it is
not a case for deleting them: mutmut injects small local faults, while
the defects the oracles have actually found are disagreements about
meaning (a `":"` filter fragment folding to the empty string; #255's
accent folding). The arm also ran at 1x Hypothesis width, which is most
of their search switched off — CLAUDE.md already records that both
defects in !249's second commit were invisible at default width.
`docs/design/mutation-evidence.md` states all of that, plus the
run-to-run variance (454/0 timeouts vs 440/13) that makes the
properties' number a slight under-estimate.

Two bugs in the tool, both caught before the number was believed. It
first inferred kills as the complement of `mutmut results`, which cannot
tell "both killed it" from "neither listed it" — it printed `killed by
every arm 0` beside an arm that had killed 454. `results --all` gives
every verdict by name, except that `--all` is an option with a default
rather than a flag, so bare `--all` is a parse error and the listing
came back empty.

Scope: #304 named three modules #306 had already deleted. The work moved
into `_expr.py`, `_compile.py`, `_joins.py` and `_common.py` — 1,332
statements against the issue's 1,402 — and those are now in
`only_mutate`, taking it to 2,300. `accessors.py`, `query.py` and
`_pushdown.py` are held back deliberately.

What remains of #304 is written down rather than left as an absence:
full-scope wall-clock (the old 4.5 h figure is void, it was the #303
timeout artefact), per-module baselines for the three new modules, and
the 196-mutant triage.
