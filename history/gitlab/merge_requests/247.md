Rounds out the strategies with the half they never covered: the generative strategies only produce *valid* artefacts, so the readers — which sit directly on lxml and msgspec — had never been tested on a byte that was not.

Four targets: byte mutation of the fixture corpus, structure-aware mutation that keeps the document well-formed while making it invalid SDMX, the same over our own writer output, and fixed pins for the classic parser attacks.

**Hypothesis rather than Atheris, and not on preference.** atheris 3.1.0 ships three wheels — `cp312`/`cp313`/`cp314`, all `manylinux2014_x86_64` — and no sdist, so it cannot be installed on the machine where `just ci` runs. Hypothesis also shrinks a failure to a minimal payload, which for a parser is worth more than coverage guidance.

## The contract

`parse` documented `ValueError` and raised five types, four of them third-party. It now raises `SdmxParseError` (a `ValueError`, with the original as `__cause__`); `SdmxConformanceError` becomes a subclass.

## What the fuzzer found

Nine leaks, all one shape — a reader converting document text with a builtin that raises bare `ValueError`: `SdmxUrn.parse`/`make` on a corrupted URN or blank id; `int()` on six different attributes; `float()` on TextFormat bounds; `fromisoformat()` on validity and reference periods; two enum literals; `etree.QName` on `<mes:>` (lxml reports that as a bare ValueError, not an LxmlError); and both metadata readers on a MetadataSet naming no flow.

They arrived one family at a time, each found only after the previous was fixed — which is the argument for `formats/_coerce.py` over a `try` at each site.

## And the hardening we already had

sdmxlib is immune to billion laughs, XXE and deep nesting — immune **by inheritance**, from lxml's defaults and msgspec's typed decode, with nothing in the suite saying so. One `XMLParser(resolve_entities=True)` would undo all three silently. Now pinned.

Runs in under two seconds in `just test`; scales 20x under `just test-properties`.
