Six consecutive clean runs at the deep profile (2000 examples per property) prompted the question the pass count cannot answer: is the fuzzer still reaching anything, or has it plateaued?

Measuring that turned up a wrong claim in the code just merged. The docstrings said *"roughly three quarters of byte mutations die inside the XML parser, so the reader never runs"* — a figure from the throwaway probe that opened #247, which used an ad-hoc mutation distribution rather than the one that shipped. Measured over 3000 examples of the real strategy, byte mutation gets **39%** of its payloads past the parser, and 76% reach our reader one way or another. Backwards.

| strategy | parses | recovers >=1 | recovers >=10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| byte mutation (XML) | 38.6% | 29.4% | 8.8% |
| structure-aware (XML) | 92.6% | 67.3% | 24.3% |
| byte mutation (JSON) | 29.5% | 29.3% | - |
| structure-aware (JSON) | 46.7% | 45.7% | - |

The measurement does support what the sentence was reaching for: structure-aware mutation gets about three times as deep, and byte mutation is not a weaker version of it — a quarter of its payloads are refused by lxml or msgspec first, exercising the boundary rather than the reader.

Docstrings only; no behaviour change.
