Four of `CompileContext`'s six fields were dead, for three different reasons. The issue asked for *reasons rather than deletions*, since dormant and broken look identical from outside: the next reader either prunes something one feature away from mattering, or assumes `join_sqls` works and finds it silently empty.

The decisive change: the issue says the accumulators revive if #259 implements annotation-valued sorting. **#259 closed that path instead**, deliberately — an annotation type may appear many times on one row, so there is no scalar to order by. Shut by design, not pending.

## The contract was understating an asymmetry

`Predicate` promised "one SQL with shared joins". Since #264 that is true of `code_query` and false of the other three, which **raise** rather than drop a join they cannot splice. A pinaxlib author following the old wording would have hit `NotImplementedError` from three of four compilers. Now stated, with why the promise narrowed.

## Two guards, because prose rots

- **The live half was untested.** #264 covered the refusal; that `code_query` genuinely splices a foreign join — right place, join binds before where binds — was asserted nowhere, and that is the half downstream depends on.
- **The dead half is a static AST inventory.** If `next_alias` gains a caller, a docstring reading "never called, in its entire history" becomes false silently. Matching is on attribute access, not on lines *containing* the name — a first draft flagged two comments mentioning `join_aliases` historically, and a guard that fires on prose is a guard that gets deleted.

Both verified discriminating.

It cost fifteen minutes here, presenting as a parameter-ordering bug that did not exist — `git diff` clean, `inspect.getsource` correct, behaviour old. The tell is a probe whose *restore* step fails.
