## Two findings in the hop I had never measured

3.0 ↔ 3.1 are adjacent versions, so this is where I least expected losses. Both were silent:

**`DataConstraint/@role` is `use="required"` in 3.0 and does not exist in 3.1.** The writer dropped it and the reader supplied the default, so a constraint asserting `Actual` came back asserting **`Allowed`**. That is not a missing field — it is a *false statement*. The document is schema-conformant and claims something the input did not.

**`<ReleaseCalendar>` is on 3.0's `DataConstraintBaseType` and absent from 3.1**, its timing semantics having moved to `AvailabilityConstraint`, which sdmxlib does not model.

Both are now refused. The first especially: losing data is bad, and quietly changing what a document *claims* is worse. Neither was reachable from the same-version harness, because each format was individually self-consistent — only the hop exposes them.

## Controls

Five mutations verified to fail, including **both directions of staleness**: a `_BLOCKED` entry for a hop that actually works, and a `_LOSSY` entry for a hop that is actually clean. Plus a typo'd exemption key, which must not silently exempt nothing.

There is also a canary asserting most 3.0 ↔ 3.1 hops convert — a point release blocking a quarter of its own hops would mean something structural had broken rather than one artefact gaining a constraint.
