Closes #318.

`references=` decides which relations come back; `detail=` decides how much of each artefact, and it was hard-coded to `full` — so a listing naming ids downloaded every code of every codelist.

## What a stub omits, measured

`codelist/ECB?detail=allstubs` returns 244 codelists. Compared field-by-field against a full response for the **109 present in both**, exactly two things differ: `codes` and `description`. `id`, `agency`, `version` and `name` are all present.

A third field, `maintainer`, also differed and is deliberately **not** attributed to `detail`: the full response was `references=all`, which pulled the AgencyScheme and resolved the agency's name. Attributing it would have made every query needing an agency name ask for `full` on the strength of a confounded comparison.

The saving: **88 KB** for the whole ECB codelist catalogue as stubs, against **69 KB** for `CL_CURRENCY` alone at full detail.

## Only a projection may ask for a stub

Found by wiring it and reading the emitted URLs:

    ids()     : ['CL']                     -> fine on a stub
    collect() : LazyCodelist with 0 codes  -> silently hollow

A row terminal resolves rows to *artefacts* out of the store, so a stub-populated store answers `collect()` with zero codes and no error. `projection=None` therefore means `full`, and is the default.

## A bug the URLs showed

The §9.1 rewrite narrows the outer scan by `dataflow.dsd_urn` — a reference a stub does not carry. That column is not in `step.predicates` because the **planner** produced it, so a check walking only what the caller wrote reported `allstubs`, every `dsd_urn` came back null, and the membership matched nothing.

Third occurrence of one shape: computing a fetch parameter from *part* of what will be evaluated (also `references=` from a push's own plan, and the outer scan left unbounded). Anything derived for a scan has to account for the planner's own additions, not just the caller's.

## Smaller decisions

- `columns_in` is a separate walker from `reached_paths`: a `PredicateShape` carries a column *name* but not its table, and `detail` needs both. Adding a table to the shape would have changed what a `Capability` declaration matches — a much larger blast radius than one walker.
- The stub surface is an allowlist of the **proven**, not a denylist of the known-absent. An unchecked column costs a `full` fetch rather than a silent empty result.
