`test_collect_stays_close_to_frame` was the only assertion in the bundle tier
that measured time, and since #390 put that tier in `just ci` it was the only
one that could fail for a reason unrelated to the code. It did: 22.7x against
a 16x bound with 12.9x measured, then green alone and green on two further
full-tier runs.

Its own defence was that a *ratio* is stable across machines while a ceiling
on a 2.6 GB bundle is a statement about the laptop. True of a systematically
slower machine, false of contention within one run — the two sides are timed
at different moments and the tier does two minutes of heavy DuckDB work around
them, so one stall moves the ratio.

## What replaces it

`collect()` builds one object per row and `frame()` builds none, so the
construction tax is the calls `collect()` makes that `frame()` does not,
divided by rows:

| | `frame()` | `collect()` | `collect(annotations=False)` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `q.code` 579,951 | 838 calls | +26.14/row | +15.33/row |
| `q.hcode` 499,143 | 843 calls | +35.00/row | +35.00/row |

Identical across three runs, to the second decimal, on a machine doing other
things. Bounds sit ~30 % above, per accessor — a `Code` is cheaper to build
than a `HierarchicalCode`, and `q.hcode`'s two figures coincide because those
nodes carry almost no annotations while half of `q.code`'s collect is the
annotation read.

Sabotage-checked: six trivial calls a row takes `q.code` from 15.33 to 21.14
and trips the bound.

## And what licenses `frame()` as the baseline

`test_frame_builds_nothing_per_row` — new. The test above subtracts `frame()`'s
calls and divides by rows, which is only a construction tax while `frame()`
itself does no per-row work. It does not (838 calls for 579,951 rows), but that
is a property of the polars path rather than a guarantee, and if it regained
one the subtraction would quietly cancel the cost it exists to measure. Bounded
flat at 5,000 rather than per row, because the claim is exactly that the number
does not scale. Sabotage-checked: one call per row reports 1.002 per row.

## Side effects

The wall figures are not lost — `test_query_codes` and
`test_query_hierarchy_nodes` still report them under `just bench-compare`,
which is where a number that varies with the machine belongs. A call count
cannot see work that gets slower without getting more numerous, and the
docstring says so rather than implying the guard is total.

`_timed` and the two `COLLECT_OVER_FRAME` constants are gone with their only
caller. The tier is also **~15 % faster** — 113 s against 128 s — because
min-of-three wall timing ran each terminal nine times per case and this profiles
three.

Three consecutive full-tier runs green, 23 assertions rather than 21.

Closes #401.
